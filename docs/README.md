# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

OctoAcme uses a simple, customer-first project lifecycle that moves from initiation to planning, execution, release, and retrospective. At the start of a project, the team confirms the business need, defines measurable outcomes, identifies stakeholders, and captures the initial timeline, risks, and resource needs in a one-pager. Once approved, work is broken into a prioritized backlog with clear acceptance criteria, milestones, dependencies, and a Definition of Done so the team can plan and deliver in small, testable increments.

During execution, work is tracked on a project board and moved through stages such as Backlog, Ready, In Progress, In Review, QA, and Done. Clear roles help keep delivery organized: the Project Manager coordinates schedules, risks, communication, and dependencies; the Product Manager sets the vision and prioritization; developers build and test; QA validates quality and acceptance; and stakeholders provide input and approvals. Team rhythm includes standups, weekly syncs, demos, and escalation paths when blockers emerge, ensuring progress and risks are visible to the right people at the right time.

Communication is a core part of the process. OctoAcme emphasizes regular updates, a single source of truth for status, and proactive risk management. Weekly PM and product alignment, delivery standups, milestone reviews, and stakeholder updates all help maintain transparency and alignment. Risks are tracked with impact/likelihood, owners, and mitigation plans, and escalation follows a clear path from the team to the PM, Product Lead, and sponsor when needed. This keeps issues visible early and reduces the chance of surprises during delivery.

Quality and release readiness are built into each phase. New functionality should include tests, CI should validate code quality and security, and critical user flows should get smoke tests before deployment. Releases require acceptance criteria to be met, security checks to pass, rollback plans to be documented, and post-deployment verification to confirm the change is working as expected. After each sprint or milestone, the team conducts a retrospective to capture lessons, assign action items, and turn improvements into backlog work. This creates a cycle of continuous improvement while keeping customer value, velocity, and delivery discipline at the center of project execution.
